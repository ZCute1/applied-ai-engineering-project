## Consistency is the whole point

Most medications are prescribed at fixed intervals — once daily, or every twelve
hours — and they work best when those intervals are actually kept. A medication
task is one of the few things on a pet's schedule that should not be moved around
to make room for something else.

## Attach it to something you already do

The reliable way to remember a daily dose is to pin it to a routine that already
happens, usually a meal. "With breakfast" is far easier to keep up over months
than a time in isolation.

## Twice-daily means roughly twelve hours apart

Two doses squeezed into the same part of the day are not the same as two doses
spread across it. If a morning dose goes with breakfast, the second belongs with
the evening meal rather than late at night.

## Follow the label, not a schedule app

Whether a medication needs food, must be given at a strict time, or can flex by an
hour comes from the prescribing vet and the label. If a dose is missed, ask the vet
what to do rather than doubling up — and finish a course of antibiotics even after
the animal looks better.
