## They eat more often

Young animals need feeding three or four times a day rather than twice, in small
portions, because they cannot hold enough in one meal to fuel how fast they are
growing. Most move to adult meal patterns somewhere around six months, on the
vet's advice.

## Toilet breaks are frequent

A young puppy needs out very often — after waking, after eating, after play, and
several times in between. A rough guide is that a puppy can hold on for about one
hour per month of age, so these are short, frequent tasks rather than something to
batch.

## Little and often for exercise

Formal exercise should be modest while joints are still developing, and long forced
walks are worse than free play in short bursts. Puppies and kittens also sleep an
enormous amount — 16 to 20 hours a day — so a schedule packed edge to edge with
activity works against them.

## Sleep and handling

Deliberate quiet time matters as much as activity: an overtired puppy behaves much
like an overtired toddler. Short daily handling sessions — paws, ears, mouth,
brushing — while they are young make grooming and vet visits far easier later.
Check vaccination and worming timing with your vet rather than guessing.
