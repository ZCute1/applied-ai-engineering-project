## Short sessions, several times a day

Cats hunt in brief bursts rather than long stretches. Two or three play sessions
of 10 to 15 minutes suit them far better than one 40-minute session, which most
cats simply walk away from partway through.

## Play like prey

A wand toy moved like an animal — darting, pausing, hiding behind furniture — gets
a response that a toy dragged in a straight line does not. Let the cat catch the
toy at the end; a hunt that never succeeds is frustrating rather than satisfying.

## Dawn and dusk

Cats are crepuscular, most active around dawn and dusk. Play scheduled in the
early morning or the hours before bed matches the cat's own rhythm, and an evening
session followed by a meal often means a calmer night for everyone.

## Signs of boredom

Overgrooming, night-time yowling, ambushing ankles, and picking fights with other
pets in the house are common signs an indoor cat is under-stimulated. Vertical
space, window perches, and food puzzles help between play sessions.
