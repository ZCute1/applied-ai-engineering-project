## Shorter and more often

A low-energy or older dog usually does better with 20 to 30 minutes of gentle
walking a couple of times a day than with one demanding outing. Stiffness after
too much activity often shows up the next morning rather than the same evening,
so it is easy to overshoot without noticing.

## Warm up first

Older dogs and dogs with joint trouble move better once they have been up and
about for a while. A walk scheduled shortly after the dog wakes is harder on stiff
joints than the same walk an hour later, so mid-morning or afternoon slots tend to
suit them better.

## Keep the mind busy

Activity that does not stress joints still matters: gentle sniffing walks, food
puzzles, and short low-impact training. Ten minutes of this is often more welcome
than an extra ten minutes of walking.

## Watch for change

A dog that suddenly wants less exercise than usual, or that lags on familiar
routes, is worth a vet visit rather than a lighter schedule. Slowing down over
years is normal; slowing down over weeks is not.
