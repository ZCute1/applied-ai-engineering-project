## Avoid the middle of the day

In hot weather, walks belong in the early morning and the later evening. The hours
either side of midday are the hottest and carry the most risk, so a summer routine
usually means shifting the day's exercise outward rather than shortening it.

## Check the ground

Pavement and asphalt hold heat long after the air has started to cool, and can
burn paw pads. The back of your hand held to the ground for seven seconds is the
usual test: if you cannot keep it there comfortably, it is too hot for paws.

## Shorten and slow down

On a genuinely hot day, trade distance for shade and sniffing. Flat-faced breeds,
older dogs, overweight dogs, and thick-coated dogs overheat much faster than
others and may need to skip the walk entirely rather than do a shorter one.

## Warning signs

Heavy frantic panting, drooling, bright red gums, wobbliness, or vomiting mean
stop immediately, get the dog somewhere cool, offer water, and call a vet.
Heatstroke is an emergency and can develop within minutes.
